# pseudo-jepa, v2
<!-- id: v2 · chapter: Overview · scene: v2-overview · map: all -->

> **Trained.** The left panel is one real v2 forward pass: the same dog photo as v1, the frozen encoders, and the predictor after its 30th and last epoch. The numbers, and two demos, are on the Results tab.

v2 keeps v1's idea of a predictor with an accumulator q, but swaps both from-scratch encoders for frozen, pretrained ones and trains only the predictor. That puts it next to Meta's **VL-JEPA** (Chen et al., 2025, [arXiv 2512.10942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10942)), which is built from the same two encoders.

| | **v1** | **v2** | **VL-JEPA** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Learns by | picking the right caption out of 256 | filling in a hidden photo's caption embedding | predicting the text's embedding, given video and a question |
| Trained | everything, 14.1M | the predictor only, 11.3M | predictor + text encoder, about 790M |

## The photo goes in at 256 pixels
<!-- id: v2-input · chapter: Image · scene: v2-input · map: image -->

The photo is resized and cropped to 256 × 256 and cut into 16 × 16 patches of 16 pixels. V-JEPA 2 is a video model, so the photo is shown to it twice, as a two-frame clip.

| | **v1** | **v2** | **VL-JEPA** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Photo | 128 px, random crops and colour jitter | 256 px, random flips | 256 px, frames of video; a photo is copied into every frame |

## Hiding: four blocks
<!-- id: v2-mask · scene: v2-mask · map: image -->

The 256 patches are grouped into an 8 × 8 grid of cells, 2 × 2 patches each. Four rectangles of 15–20% of that grid are hidden. They overlap, so on average about two-thirds of the photo disappears; this draw left 33 of 64 cells.

| | **v1** | **v2** | **VL-JEPA** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hidden while training | nothing | 4 blocks, I-JEPA's sampler; the whole photo at test time | nothing |

## The frozen encoder sees only what is left
<!-- id: v2-encode · scene: v2-encode · map: image-blocks -->

The hidden patches are dropped right after the patch embedding, so V-JEPA 2's 24 layers never see them: the visible tokens can't peek at the holes through attention. The heat on the left is how strongly each visible patch fires, layer by layer.

The encoder is frozen. Nothing in it changes during training, so the hidden parts can only be guessed downstream.

| | **v1** | **v2** | **VL-JEPA** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Image encoder | from scratch: 4 conv + 6 Transformer blocks | V-JEPA 2 ViT-L (304M), **frozen** | V-JEPA 2 ViT-L (304M), **frozen** |

## Back to cells
<!-- id: v2-pool · scene: v2-pool · map: image-encoder -->

Each visible cell's four patch vectors are averaged into one. Out come 33 tokens of 1,024 numbers, each still knowing which cell it came from.

## The caption side: five captions, one target
<!-- id: v2-text · chapter: Text · scene: v2-text · map: target -->

All five captions go through EmbeddingGemma, a frozen 300M-parameter text model. It cuts text into word pieces, not whole words, so it never meets an unknown word. Each caption becomes 768 numbers, and the target is the **average of the five**.

| | **v1** | **v2** | **VL-JEPA** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Text encoder | from scratch, 7,441-word vocabulary, trained | EmbeddingGemma-300M, **frozen** | EmbeddingGemma-300M, **trained** at 0.05 × the learning rate |
| Target | one caption, picked at random | the average of 5: blends their wording, so it never equals any single one | the given text |

## The predictor and its accumulator q
<!-- id: v2-predictor · chapter: Predictor · scene: v2-predictor · map: predictor -->

The 33 cell tokens are projected from 1,024 down to 384 numbers, and each gets a fixed sin-cos code for its row and column. q goes in front, then six Transformer blocks. The heat shows where q looks in each block. Only q is kept: `LayerNorm` → `Linear(384, 768)`.

| | **v1** | **v2** | **VL-JEPA** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Predictor | 2 blocks, width 256 | 6 blocks, width 384 | last 8 layers of Llama-3.2-1B |
| Reads | the photo | the visible part of the photo | the video **and a text question**, so it can answer, not just caption |

## The loss: how far off, nothing else
<!-- id: v2-loss · chapter: Loss · scene: v2-loss · map: loss -->

Subtract the guess from the target, square every difference, add them up. No table of 256 captions, no wrong answers.

| | **v1** | **v2** | **VL-JEPA** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loss | InfoNCE, both directions | **squared distance** to the target | InfoNCE, both directions |
| Why it can't collapse | 255 wrong captions in every batch | the target comes from a frozen encoder and cannot move toward the guess | wrong answers in every batch |
| The JEPA part | a predictor | predict a hidden input's representation, no negatives | a predictor in embedding space; still contrastive |

## Scale
<!-- id: v2-scale · chapter: Scale · scene: v2-scale · map: all -->

| | **v1** | **v2** | **VL-JEPA** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data | Flickr30k, 29,000 photos | Flickr30k, 29,000 photos | Datacomp, YFCC-100M, Action100M, then 25M+ fine-tuning samples |
| Seen | 5.8M pairs (200 epochs) | 0.9M (30 epochs) | 3.3–3.6B |
| Batch × steps | 256 × 22,600 | 512 × 1,680 | 24,000 × 100,000 for photos alone, then video |
| Hardware | one Mac, 3.4 hours | one Mac, about 4 s a step | 192 H200s, about 4 weeks |
